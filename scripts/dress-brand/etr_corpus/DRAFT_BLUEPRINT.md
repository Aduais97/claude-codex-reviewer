## PAGE 1 — POSITION & OFFER

### Where you sit
Clio Peppiatt sells hand-beaded event dresses at roughly NZ$3,500–$10,000+
(minis ≈ US$2,135–2,620, gowns to US$6,250 — Net-a-Porter, Oct 2026). Bella
Hadid and the It-girl circuit wear it, which means millions of women crave the
look and almost none can buy it. You sell that craving at a price they CAN pay,
shipped from NZ. You're right: you are not competition to Clio — you serve the
audience her press creates. That audience is already searching: "Clio Peppiatt
dupe" listicles exist and rank (Lane Creatore runs an 18-dress roundup).

### The one rule that protects everything: sell the look, never the name
- Your own channels (site, ads, captions, hashtags) never say "Clio Peppiatt"
  or "dupe". Reasons: Fair Trading Act 1986 s9/s13/s12A, Trade Marks Act 2002
  s89, and Meta/TikTok ad policies that reject dupe claims even when lawful.
- Others CAN say it. Creators, customers, and dupe listicles calling you "the
  Clio Peppiatt look for $X" is earned media — pitch the listicle writers,
  never publish the comparison yourself.
- Copy the THEME (hand-beading, playful motifs, event energy), never a
  specific identifiable dress — NZ copyright can protect garment designs for
  ~16 years. Original motifs in the same language = safe lane + a real brand.
- Vocabulary that captures the search intent legally: "hand-beaded mini
  dress", "embellished event dress", "beaded birthday dress", "It-girl beaded
  dress NZ". These are the terms Pinterest/Google/TikTok searchers actually use.

### The Grand Slam Offer (Value Equation: ↑dream × ↑likelihood ÷ ↓time ÷ ↓effort)
| Lever | What you do |
|---|---|
| Dream outcome | "The dress everyone asks about." Shoot on real bodies, at real events, in motion — beads need video light. |
| Likelihood | Reviews + tagged customer photos on every product page; macro video of the actual beadwork (proof it's not an AliExpress flat-sequin print). |
| Time delay | NZ-based = days, not the 3–6 week AliExpress wait. Say it everywhere: "Ships from Auckland." |
| Effort & sacrifice | Honest fit: beaded dresses are heavy — publish garment weight, stretch, and height-on-model. Free easy exchanges. |

**Offer stack (first drop):**
1. **Core:** the dress, numbered small-batch drop ("1 of 30").
2. **Bonuses (stack value past price):** matching spare-bead repair kit (solves
   the #1 fear of beaded garments), styling card, care guide.
3. **Guarantee:** "Fits-or-swap" — free exchange, and if we can't fit you, full
   refund. (Conditional guarantee per Hormozi; protects margin vs blanket refunds.)
4. **Scarcity (real):** 30 units, numbered. Sold out = waitlist, never restock
   the same motif.
5. **Urgency:** drop window opens Thursday 7pm NZT, closes Sunday or sell-out.
6. **MAGIC name the drop,** not the discount — e.g. "THE MAIN CHARACTER DROP —
   30 hand-beaded dresses for summer's big nights."

Price: hold a premium-accessible corridor (the dupe buyer still wants to feel
luxury). Anchor against the original: "the look is $4,000 — yours is $XXX."
Test price on drop 1; raise, never discount (add bonuses instead).

## PAGE 2 — THE GROWTH ENGINE (Core Four, NZ-tuned)

### 1. CONTENT — your primary weapon (give:ask 3.5:1)
The beadwork IS the content moat. Hand-craft process video is top-performing
fashion content, and competitors who drop-ship flat sequin prints cannot film it.
- **Platforms:** TikTok + IG Reels (same 9:16 asset), Pinterest (dresses are
  Pinterest's home turf; pins compound as search traffic for years).
- **Pillars:** 40% process (macro beading, bead trays, motif design, time-lapse);
  30% try-on/styling (one dress, three events); 20% drop BTS + reveals;
  10% reposted customer content.
- **Rule of 100:** 100 minutes/day on content — film in batches, post 1–2×/day
  on TikTok, 4–5 Reels/week, 5–10 pins/week.
- **Never AI-generate product video.** AI smears fine detail — beadwork turns to
  mush exactly like logotypes do (proven in this family's prior brand build).
  Real macro footage is the one asset competitors can't fake.

### 2. WARM OUTREACH — NZ is a 2-degree market
Ball season, graduations, weddings, Cup/Derby race days: everyone knows someone
who needs THE dress. Every sale: ask for tagged photos at the event ("tag us,
we repost"). Personally message every follower who engages twice.

### 3. SEEDING & COLD OUTREACH (the exposure multiplier)
- Gift or loan dresses to 10–20 NZ/AU micro-creators (5k–50k followers) timed
  to real events they're attending — content-rights agreed in writing.
- DM stylists, event photographers, and ball/formal pages.
- Pitch the dupe-listicle writers and "affordable version of" creators: THEY
  make the comparison, you supply photos and a discount code.

### 4. THE NZ CHEAT CODE — Designer Wardrobe
List last-drop pieces for rental on Designer Wardrobe (consignment or lend).
Occasion-wear shoppers in NZ start there; every rental is a paid try-on that
walks your dress through a real event and someone else's photos. Hold current
drops back so full-price buyers aren't undercut (standard NZ-label practice).

### PAID ADS — last, not first (More/Better/New)
Do MORE of the organic format that wins, make it BETTER, and only then put
$10–20/day behind the proven winner as a Spark Ad/boost. Never cold-launch paid
on untested creative — and never with dupe language (instant rejection risk).

## PAGE 3 — 90-DAY ROADMAP, SCOREBOARD, GUARDRAILS

### Phase 0 — Foundations (weeks 1–2)
- Shoot real-model photo + macro video set (beads in motion, golden light).
- Product pages: garment weight, stretch, height-on-model, size chart, exchange
  policy. Honest fit copy kills returns and FTA risk in one move.
- Sweep every caption/ad/hashtag for designer names and "dupe". Fix now.
- Set up Pinterest business + TikTok business accounts; email capture on site.

### Phase 1 — Engine on (weeks 3–6)
- Daily content per the pillar mix. First numbered drop with full offer stack.
- Seed 10 creators around one real event cluster (ball season / race day).
- List 2–3 past pieces on Designer Wardrobe. Pitch 3 dupe-listicle writers.

### Phase 2 — More/Better/New (weeks 7–12)
- Double down on the winning content format (More), upgrade its production
  (Better), only then test: AU shipping, paid boost on the proven winner,
  second drop (New).
- Email list is the escape from the algorithm: drop announcements go to email
  first ("the list shops before TikTok hears about it").

### Scoreboard — 6 numbers weekly (followers are vanity; these aren't)
| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Saves + shares per post | The algorithm's real currency |
| Email signups | Owned audience, drop demand signal |
| Site sessions | Exposure actually landing |
| Conversion rate on drop days | Offer strength |
| UGC/tagged photos count | Social proof engine health |
| Sell-through % per drop | Scarcity honesty + buying signal |

### Guardrails (non-negotiable)
1. No designer names or "dupe" in owned media, ads, hashtags, SEO tags — ever.
2. Never copy one identifiable dress; always your own motif in the shared language.
3. No AI-generated product video; real beadwork macro only.
4. Scarcity must be real — never restock a "sold out" numbered motif.
5. Never state a stat or claim you can't source (FTA s12A bites unsubstantiated
   representations — including "hand-beaded" if any part is machine-made).
