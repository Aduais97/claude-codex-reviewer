## PAGE 1 — WHERE YOU SIT & THE OFFER

### Where you sit
Clio Peppiatt sells hand-beaded event dresses at roughly NZ$3,500–$10,000+
(minis ≈ US$2,135–2,620, gowns to US$6,250 — Net-a-Porter, Oct 2026), and
Bella Hadid wears it (Vogue/AOL coverage, 2025–26). The demand that press
creates vastly outstrips the number who can buy — and it's searchable:
"Clio Peppiatt dupe" listicles already exist and rank. You aren't her
competition — you serve the audience her press creates, at a price they can
pay, shipped from NZ.

### The one rule that protects everything: sell the look, never the name
- Your own channels (site, ads, captions, hashtags) never say "Clio Peppiatt"
  or "dupe" — Fair Trading Act 1986 s9/s13/s12A, Trade Marks Act 2002 s89, and
  Meta/TikTok ad policies that reject dupe claims even when lawful.
- Others CAN say it. Creators and listicles calling you "the look for $X" is
  earned media — pitch the writers; never publish the comparison yourself.
- Copy the THEME (hand-beading, playful motifs, event energy), never a specific
  identifiable dress — NZ copyright can protect garment designs for ~16 years.
- Capture the search intent legally: "hand-beaded mini dress", "embellished
  event dress", "beaded birthday dress NZ". These phrases are also your SEO —
  product titles, collection names ("Beaded Mini Dresses NZ"), page titles,
  image alt text; Phase 0 connects Google Search Console + Google Shopping.

### The Grand Slam Offer (Value Equation: ↑dream × ↑likelihood ÷ ↓time ÷ ↓effort)
| Lever | What you do |
|---|---|
| Dream outcome | "The dress everyone asks about." Shoot on real bodies, at real events, in motion — beads need video light. |
| Likelihood | Reviews + tagged customer photos on every product page; macro video of the actual beadwork — proof it's not a flat machine-sequin print. |
| Time delay | If stocked: "Ships from Auckland — X working days NZ-wide" (the real, timed X). If made-to-order: sell the making — "hand-finished to your order" — and publish the true lead time. |
| Effort & sacrifice | Honest fit: beaded dresses are heavy — publish garment weight, stretch, and height-on-model. Free easy exchanges. |

**Offer stack (first drop):**
1. **Core:** the dress, numbered small-batch drop ("1 of 30").
2. **Bonuses:** matching spare-bead repair kit (solves the obvious fear of
   beaded garments — lost beads), styling card, care guide.
3. **Guarantee:** "Fits-or-swap" — free exchange, and if we can't fit you, full
   refund — in addition to your Consumer Guarantees Act rights.
4. **Scarcity (real):** 30 units, numbered. Sold out = waitlist; retire the

5. **Urgency:** drop window opens Thursday 7pm NZT, closes Sunday or sell-out.
6. **Name the drop, not a discount:** "THE MAIN CHARACTER DROP — 30 hand-beaded
   dresses for your biggest nights of summer."

**Price:** hold a premium-accessible corridor — hand-beaded designer event
dresses retail at roughly NZ$3,500–$10,000+ (Net-a-Porter, Oct 2026); yours
is $XXX. Test on drop 1; raise, never discount — codes add a bonus, never cut
price.

## PAGE 2 — THE GROWTH ENGINE (Core Four, NZ-tuned)

### 1. CONTENT — your primary weapon (give : ask = 3.5 : 1)
- **Platforms:** TikTok + IG Reels (same 9:16 asset), Pinterest (dresses are
  Pinterest's home turf; Pinterest is search, not feed — pins keep surfacing
  long after posting).
- **Pillars:** 40% process (macro beading, bead trays, motif design,
  time-lapse); 30% try-on/styling (one dress, three events); 20% drop BTS +
  reveals; 10% screened customer reposts. *(If beading isn't done in-house:
  swap the process pillar for macro QC, bead-repair and styling content, and
  claim "hand-beaded" only if substantiated for every unit — never "hand-beaded
  by us".)* If the gate passes, process video is the one asset drop-ship
  competitors cannot film.
- **Rule of 100:** 100 minutes/day on content — film in batches, post 1–2×/day
  on TikTok, 4–5 Reels/week, 5–10 pins/week. Floor: 5 TikToks, 3 Reels, 5 pins
  per week — never below, never a zero-week.
- **Never AI-generate product video.** AI smears fine detail — beadwork turns
  to mush exactly like logotypes do (proven in this family's prior brand
  build). Real macro footage is the asset competitors can't fake.

### 2. WARM OUTREACH — NZ is a 2-degree market
Ball season, graduations, weddings, race days: everyone knows someone who needs
THE dress. Every sale: ask for tagged photos at the event ("tag us, we repost"
— screened per Guardrail 2 before anything is amplified).

### 3. SEEDING & AFFILIATES (the exposure multiplier)
- Hold back 3–4 sample dresses (outside the numbered run) as a loan pool;
  rotate across 8–10 creators per event cluster (plus stylists, event
  photographers and ball/formal pages); gift only to the single best performer
  after results. Content-rights agreed in writing; their own disclosure
  (#gifted/#ad) and their own words — never supply comparison copy.
- Stand up Shopify Collabs (or equivalent): 10–15% commission plus a reader
  code that adds a bonus (spare-bead kit) rather than cutting price. Pitch
  dupe-listicle writers with photos, facts and the affiliate link — their
  words, their disclosure.

### 4. PAID ADS — last, not first (More/Better/New)
Do MORE of the organic format that wins, make it BETTER, and only then put
$10–20/day behind the proven winner. Paid spend runs only on brand-made,
name-clean creative — never boost or Spark-Ad a third-party comparison video.

### THE NZ PARTNER PLAY — Designer Wardrobe
List last-drop pieces for rental on Designer Wardrobe — NZ's dominant
occasion-wear rental/resale platform (Spinoff, 2022). Every rental is a paid
try-on that walks your dress through a real event and someone else's photos.
Hold current drops back so full-price buyers aren't undercut (standard NZ-label
practice). Listing copy follows Guardrail 1 — no designer names, no "dupe",
on DW or any third-party platform.

## PAGE 3 — 90-DAY ROADMAP, SCOREBOARD, GUARDRAILS

### Phase 0 — Foundations (weeks 1–2)
- Confirm the production story (where/how beading happens, is it filmable) —
  it gates the content pillar and every "hand-beaded" claim.
- Shoot real-model photo + macro video set (beads in motion, golden light).
- Product pages: garment weight, stretch, height-on-model, size chart,
  exchange policy. Honest fit copy kills returns and FTA risk in one move.
- Checkout: enable Afterpay and/or Zip plus Shop Pay before drop 1; switch on
  AU (ideally US/UK) shipping zones with real NZ Post international rates —
  marketing stays NZ-first, but the checkout must be able to serve a listicle
  reader anywhere.
- Google Search Console + free Google Shopping listings; email capture on site;
  TikTok creator account (full audio library — add Business when Spark Ads
  begin); sweep every caption/ad/hashtag for designer names and "dupe".

### Phase 1 — Engine on (weeks 3–6)
Content (100 min/day) + warm outreach + drop 1 with the full offer stack —
nothing else. One channel done to competence before the next switches on.

### Phase 1.5 — First seeding wave (weeks 5–8)
5 NZ creators from the loan pool around one real event cluster (ball season /
race day). Measure before widening.

### Phase 2 — More/Better/New (weeks 7–12)
Designer Wardrobe listings · listicle/affiliate pitches (international only
once international checkout is live) · AU creator seeding · $10–20/day paid
boost on the proven organic winner · drop 2. Email list is the escape from the
algorithm: drops open to the list first.

### Guardrails (non-negotiable)
1. No designer names or "dupe" in anything we own, publish, amplify or tag;
   never copy one identifiable dress — own motifs in the shared language, and
   keep a dated motif-development file (sketches, references, iterations) as
   evidence of independent design.
2. Screen before amplifying: never repost, whitelist or boost third-party
   content containing a designer name or "dupe". Paid runs only on brand-made,
   name-clean creative. Anything gifted, paid or coded carries the creator's
   own disclosure (#gifted/#ad) and their own words.
3. No AI-generated product video; real beadwork macro only.
4. Scarcity must be real — retired motifs stay retired; variants are announced
   as variants.
5. Never state a claim you can't substantiate (FTA s12A) — including
   "hand-beaded" if any part is machine-made, and any delivery-speed promise
   you haven't timed.
