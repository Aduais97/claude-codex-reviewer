## B. METHODOLOGY

1. **Evidence discipline.** Every finding cites: draft clause number; the corpus document and clause/page it is tested against. No finding without a corpus citation. Facts asserted from outside the corpus (e.g. case law, statute) are labelled as legal context, not corpus evidence.
2. **Severity rubric** (apply consistently):
   - **BLOCKER** — factually wrong against `deal_facts.txt`, legally defective, self-contradictory, or would fail/backfire if signed as-is (including anything that could be read as concealing regulatory matters).
   - **RECOMMENDED** — a careful NZ commercial solicitor would add or amend it; the agreement functions without it but the Purchaser is materially less protected.
   - **OPTIONAL** — polish, clarity, or belt-and-braces.
3. **Finding record format** (mandated): sequential number → severity → clause reference → issue (one paragraph, concrete failure scenario) → **exact suggested wording** (insert/replace text, drafted ready to paste; for deletions, state the words to strike). Findings ordered most-severe first within each dimension; consolidated list re-ordered most-severe first overall.
4. **Verification passes, in order:** (P1) field-by-field deal-term diff (§D1 method); (P2) clause-by-clause completeness walk against the Purchaser's three stated requirements (§D2); (P3) cross-reference and defined-terms audit (§D3); (P4) enforceability tests (§D4); (P5) three-way precedent comparison (§D5); (P6) adversarial pass — read the draft *as the Vendor's solicitor would attack it*, given she has inside knowledge and knows the template (§D6); (P7) placeholder sweep (§A6).
5. **Wording sources.** Prefer adapting wording already in the corpus (ProHost drafting, or the draft's own style) over inventing new house style — the document must stay consistent for the settling solicitors.

## D4. ENFORCEABILITY RISK UNDER NZ LAW (Pass P4)

**Method:** apply each legal test below to the identified clause; findings state the risk, the likely judicial treatment, and drafted mitigation. Legal authorities are cited as context (the report is a hardening pass, not a legal opinion — anything turning on unresolved facts goes to §A5).

1. **Restraint of trade — cl 10.** Test as a *vendor* restraint (greater latitude than employee restraints; goodwill purchased — Nordenfelt line; NZ courts may modify unreasonable restraints under s 83 Contract and Commercial Law Act 2017). Key questions: is 24 months × all of New Zealand supportable for a $260k consideration where the Purchaser already co-owns and runs the business (goodwill protected is real but partial)? Does the cascading structure (10.2, 10.3, 10.5) create the intended severable ladder, and is 10.5's "each combination … constitutes a separate and severable restraint" wording effective (4 periods × 3 areas = 12 combinations — state them or keep the formula)? Does the reasonableness acknowledgement in 10.5 (price + inside knowledge of clients, staff, pricing, operations) recite the *right* factors? Dual-status risk: the Vendor was also an employee (weekly pay ~$1,923) — could she recharacterise the restraint as an employment restraint to attract stricter scrutiny, and does Background E + cl 10.1's "In consideration of the Purchase Price" sufficiently anchor it to the share sale?
2. **Penalty doctrine — cl 5, 6, 15.** Apply the *127 Hobson Street Ltd v Honey Bees Preschool Ltd* [2020] NZSC 53 legitimate-interest/proportionality test. Key questions: 12% p.a. default interest (cl 5) — within market range, but confirm it applies to *sums due*, not to acceleration of unmatured instalments; cl 6.1(b) cancellation mechanic (re-transfer against repayment less costs/damages) — verify it is compensatory, not forfeiture (contrast the Gwin cl 14.1 forfeiture the draft wisely dropped — confirm nothing forfeiture-like survives); cl 15 set-off — "the amount of the loss reasonably estimated by the Purchaser" is self-assessed: does cl 15.2 (repay + interest if arbitrator disagrees) sufficiently de-risk it from being attacked as a disguised penalty or unlawful withholding, and should the estimate require a written basis?
3. **Employment release — cl 9.2 and s 149 Employment Relations Act 2000.** A contractual release does not bar a personal grievance; only a certified record of settlement does. Key questions: is cl 9.2 merely an intention ("The parties intend…") — should signing the s 149 record be a **condition of settlement** or a cl 3.1 deliverable, with a mechanism if an MBIE mediator is not available by 1 Aug 2026? Does cl 3.1(e)'s no-claims acknowledgement overlap safely (it cannot itself release employment claims)? Does the release architecture keep clear of the regulatory carve-outs (a settlement cannot oust PSPLA processes)?
4. **Pre-emptive rights — constitutions/shareholders' agreement.** Drafting Note 6: check each company's constitution and any shareholders' agreement for pre-emption on transfer; waivers needed from the continuing shareholders (Hasibullah Nabawi 40.16%; Lockhart 15.24%). Key questions for the report: should waiver deliverables be added to cl 3.1, or a condition added? Note Hasibullah is *not a party* — the draft cannot bind him; route the obtaining of waivers to §A5. Also: does the sale of the trustee parcel (if it is the Vendor's beneficial stake) itself trigger pre-emption in favour of Ahmad *and Hasibullah* pro rata — a structural question for the solicitors (§A5).
5. **Regulatory (PSPLA/PSPPI Act 2010).** Confirm the draft *only* preserves and never limits regulatory exposure: cl 12.1(a)/12.2 disclosure carve-outs, cl 13 non-disparagement (needs express regulator carve-out? — see §D2), cl 14.1 cooperation (compellable and paid expenses — reasonable), cl 14.2 no-transfer/no-release (matches deal_facts' deliberate design). Key questions: does settlement (change of shareholding/officers of licensed security businesses) require PSPLA notification or licence-record updates, and should a mutual-notification covenant be added? Confirm **nothing in the draft could be read as concealing anything from the regulator** — this check is mandatory for every finding's suggested wording too.
6. **Cancellation and survival.** Under the CCLA cancellation regime, cancellation discharges unperformed future obligations. Key questions: if the Vendor cancels under cl 6.1(b) (Purchaser default) — do cl 10–13 restraints survive? If the Purchaser cancels under cl 7 — does cl 12 confidentiality survive? Is an express survival clause needed listing cl 4, 5, 8, 10–15, 16, 17? Candidate BLOCKER given the whole deal rationale (Background E) is the restraints.
7. **Trustee capacity — cl 1.2 / Lockhart.** If joined: does a corporate trustee need limited-recourse wording, and conversely does the Purchaser need the trustee's warranty (cl 4.1(a)–(b)) to be meaningful (trustee's right of indemnity from trust assets)? Route the trust-deed review to §A5.

## D6. PROTECTION OF THE PURCHASER — DEFERRED CONSIDERATION + VENDOR'S INSIDE KNOWLEDGE (Pass P6)

**Method:** adversarial walk-through of three scenarios, testing whether the draft's remedies actually function in each. Findings from this pass are the highest-value output of the review.

**Scenario 1 — Vendor breaches restraint *early* (before 1 Oct 2026):** set-off (cl 15) is live against up to $160k. Key questions: is the 7-day remedy notice workable for a restraint breach (a diverted client cannot be "remedied" — does the "where capable of remedy" qualifier do enough work)? Is the Purchaser's "reasonable estimate" of loss defensible without an agreed methodology (e.g. reference to the client's trailing 12-month revenue)?
