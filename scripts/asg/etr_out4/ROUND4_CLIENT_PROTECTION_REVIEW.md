# D4. Part 2 — Stress-Test of Clause 11.6

## D4.1 The insurer-of-retention drift (→ N16)

The trigger has two causal links: the client's cessation/reduction must result from *an act or omission*, and that act or omission need only be *"directly contributed to by the Vendor in any capacity"*. Nothing requires the act or omission to be wrongful, voluntary, or even the Vendor's own. Applied literally, the trigger reaches:

1. **Compliance conduct.** The Vendor goes silent on settlement day because 11.5 commands it. A client who valued the personal relationship drifts away *because of the silence*. The Vendor's omission (not contacting) directly contributed; set-off follows. The Vendor is condemned by obedience. Worse, 11.5's own second sentence — "shall do nothing which causes or **contributes** to any client ceasing…" (no "directly" qualifier at all) — makes the same compliant silence an *actual* breach, not merely a deemed one, exposing the Vendor to clause 7 remedies and damages at large. The two sentences of 11.5 contradict each other; a clause that is breached by compliance with itself cannot go to the other side.
2. **Requested conduct.** A QC/compliance finding delivered under the cl 9.4 advisory agreement at a Company's written request embarrasses a venue manager, who moves the contract. The Vendor "directly contributed in any capacity" — in the very capacity the Purchaser's own companies commissioned.
3. **The exit itself.** Selling, resigning and ceasing to be the liaison are acts of the Vendor. Every client who leaves in the window "because Nikki left" is literally within the trigger. But founder-attrition risk on a change of control is the risk the Purchaser priced and bought; converting it into a Vendor-funded guarantee turns deferred consideration into a retention warranty the deal never contained — the definition of making the Vendor an insurer of client retention.

**These three categories are also the penalty-doctrine attack surface.** Under *Honey Bees Preschool Ltd v 127 Hobson Street Ltd* [2020] NZSC 27 the question is whether the stipulated consequence is out of all proportion to the Purchaser's legitimate interest in performance. A set-off of estimated client-lifetime loss, triggered by innocent, requested or compelled conduct, is where that attack lands — and if 11.6 is struck as a penalty the Purchaser loses the *whole* no-fault layer, keeping only ordinary breach claims. Excluding the three categories (N16) removes every disproportionate application while keeping the clause's real target: departures the Vendor *brought about* — solicitation, disparagement, tip-offs, sabotage, "helpful" warnings to clients — whether provable as breaches of 11.1/11.5/12/13 or not.

**With N16 applied, the clause is defensible.** The favourable characterisation is then available and strong: this is not a penalty but a retention condition on deferred consideration — economically an earn-out/holdback: (i) the measure is compensatory ("loss reasonably estimated", 15.1, with particulars required); (ii) it is arbitrator-corrected with interest (15.2) and default-protected (15.3); (iii) it is capped naturally at the unpaid instalments ($160,000); (iv) Background E ties the consideration in substantial part to "the restraints and protections given by the Vendor in clauses 10 to 13" — which includes 11.4–11.6; and (v) the window is exactly the money-at-risk window, evidencing a legitimate interest, not in-terrorem intent.

## D4.2 Burden of proof — workable, with two aids

At the self-help stage the machinery favours the Purchaser: the Purchaser estimates, particularises (15.1's "full particulars" and "reasonable detail" of the estimate) and withholds; the Vendor's recourse is arbitration. At arbitration the practical burden lands on the Purchaser to establish (i) cessation or material reduction in the window, (ii) the Vendor-linked act or omission, and (iii) the causal link — and (iii) is the hard one: clients rarely give evidence, and real departures have mixed causes (price, service under new management, the exit itself). Three observations:

- **"Directly contributed to" is the right standard for the Purchaser** — contribution, not sole or dominant cause — and is about as low as a causation threshold can defensibly sit. It should not be lowered further; "directly" is what separates the clause from insurance.
- **Evidence will usually be documentary** — texts/calls to clients (11.5 breaches are themselves the evidence of the act), timing, and the 4.5 early-warning duty's paper trail. §D9 package note 3 recommends a CPP evidence practice (log client feedback; exit-interview any departing client) so a 15.1 notice can actually carry "full particulars".
- **Two drafting aids:** the notice-of-departure limb (N17) prevents the causation fight from being mooted by a notice period; the optional instalment-date compliance confirmation (N23) gives the Purchaser a representation to test the Vendor against.

## D4.3 Interaction with 15.1 — no double-count, no conflict, one circularity

- **Deeming is correctly scoped.** 11.6 makes the departure "a material breach of this clause 11 **for the purposes of clause 15.1**" — the fiction does not leak into clause 7 (default/cancellation), 5 (interest) or the 1.3 materiality definition generally. A departure caused by innocent-but-contributed conduct grounds set-off only; a departure caused by an *actual* breach of 11.1/11.5 additionally grounds damages and injunctive relief. Correct two-track design. **Cleared.**
- **No double-count.** Where one departure is both an actual 11.5 breach and an 11.6 event, the loss is the same loss once; 11.6's "the loss the Purchaser may set off under clause 15 **includes**…" supplements the 15.1 measure rather than duplicating it, and 15.2's true-up corrects any over-withholding with interest. **Cleared.**
- **No materiality conflict.** The deemed materiality bypasses 1.3's more-than-trivial test, but harmlessly: a cessation is inherently non-trivial and the reduction limb carries its own "materially". **Cleared** (optional threshold alignment → N24).
- **One circularity (→ N18).** 15.1 gives seven days to remedy a breach "where capable of remedy". Is a client departure remediable? Only by winning the client back — which requires client contact that 11.5 forbids without the Purchaser's written confirmation. The Vendor can argue every departure is remediable (delaying set-off past an instalment date, and 2.3 makes time of the essence for payment); the Purchaser controls whether the only remedy route is even lawful. N18 resolves the characterisation in the Purchaser's favour while expressly opening the win-back channel when the Purchaser wants it used.

### N16 — [BLOCKER] — cl 11.5 (second sentence) and cl 11.6 — the departure machinery contains no carve-out for conduct the SPA compels, conduct the Companies request, or the bare fact of the exit — the Vendor can be put in breach by compliance, and the clause drifts from retention protection into retention insurance

**Issue.** Fully reasoned at §D4.1. Three defects, one root cause: (i) 11.5's do-nothing sentence ("shall do nothing which causes or contributes to any client ceasing…") is breached by the client-losing effect of the silence 11.5's first sentence commands — a clause breached by compliance with itself; (ii) 11.6's trigger ("any act or omission directly contributed to by the Vendor in any capacity") reaches Company-requested QC work and the sale/exit itself, converting priced-in change-of-control attrition into a Vendor-funded guarantee; (iii) together these are the penalty-doctrine attack surface that could take down the whole layer. One inserted clause excludes precisely the three unintended categories and nothing else; a two-word edit to 11.6 additionally puts the Vendor's *own* acts beyond argument (as drafted, "directly contributed to by the Vendor" invites the pedantic reply that the Vendor's own act is not "contributed to" but *done*).

**Fix (two edits).**
1. In clause 11.6, replace:
   > as a result of any act or omission directly contributed to by the Vendor in any capacity,
   with:
   > as a result of any act or omission of, or directly contributed to by, the Vendor in any capacity,
2. Insert a new clause 11.7:
   > 11.7  The second sentence of clause 11.5 and clause 11.6 do not apply to a cessation or reduction of business to the extent that it results from: (a) anything done, or not done, by the Vendor in compliance with this agreement (including the first sentence of clause 11.5) or at the written request of the Purchaser or a Company (including services performed in good faith under the advisory agreement contemplated by clause 9.4); (b) the fact alone of the sale of the Shares, the ending of the Vendor's employment or engagement, or the Vendor ceasing to perform the client liaison role, without any other act or omission of the Vendor contributing to the cessation or reduction; or (c) any communication protected by clause 14.4.

   (Limb (b)'s "fact alone… without any other act or omission" is deliberate: a client leaving purely because the Vendor left is excluded, but a Vendor who amplifies his exit — "I'm going, and standards will go with me" — is squarely back inside 11.5 and 11.6. Limb (c) restates 14.4's tail locally so the exclusion set is complete on its face.)

**Why BLOCKER.** This is the one finding that is not pure defect-repair: limbs (a) and (b) *narrow the Purchaser's stated requirement* ("anything… in any capacity") in the only three places where its literal application is self-defeating. That is a decision for the Purchaser to make knowingly before the draft leaves the building — and transmitting without it sends Franklin Law a clause that contradicts itself and invites the penalty attack.

**Regulatory-integrity check:** limb (c) *widens* the regulator-communication protection's local visibility; nothing narrows any gateway. Clear.

### N17 — [RECOMMENDED] — cl 11.6 — the cessation must occur during the CPP, so late-window mischief whose effect lands after 1 October escapes the trigger

**Issue.** A Vendor act on, say, 25 September that causes a client to give 30 days' notice produces a cessation in late October — outside the CPP, therefore outside 11.6, even though the wrongdoing sat squarely inside the protected window and the final instalment (the set-off fund) has not yet been paid on 1 October. The Purchaser would be left to prove an actual breach of 11.1/11.5 instead of relying on the no-fault trigger. Catching the client's *notice or committed decision* inside the window closes the gap without extending the CPP itself.

**Fix (one edit in clause 11.6).** Replace:
> If, during the Client Protection Period, a client ceases to do business, or materially reduces its business, with either Company

with:
> If, during the Client Protection Period, a client ceases to do business, or materially reduces its business, with either Company, or gives notice of its intention to do so (in which case the cessation or reduction is treated as occurring when the notice is given),

**Regulatory-integrity check:** none implicated (and a "notice" prompted by a protected regulator communication remains excluded via 14.4 / N16 limb (c)). Clear.

### N18 — [RECOMMENDED] — cl 11.6 / cl 15.1 — resolve the remedy-window circularity: "remedying" a client departure requires client contact that 11.5 forbids

**Issue.** Fully reasoned at §D4.3. 15.1's seven-day remedy window applies to breaches "capable of remedy"; the only remedy for a departure is winning the client back, which requires contact the Vendor cannot lawfully make without the Purchaser's written confirmation. The Vendor gets a delay argument (pushing set-off past an instalment date on which time is of the essence); the Purchaser holds the key to the only cure. The characterisation should follow the control.

**Fix (append to clause 11.6, after the N16/N17 edits).**

**Regulatory-integrity check:** none implicated — the vendor-protective 15.2 true-up (interest) and 15.3 shield are untouched. Clear. ### N19 — [RECOMMENDED] — cl 9.2 — the record-of-settlement preservation names clauses 10 to 13 but not 4.3 to 4.5, inviting the argument that the certified s149 record settled pre-settlement client-conduct claims

### N20 — [RECOMMENDED] — cl 14.4 — "otherwise communicating with… any government agency" literally protects commercial solicitation of a government-agency client, overriding 11.5 and 11.1 for that client class

**Issue.** 14.4 protects "making any report, complaint, disclosure or submission to, cooperating with, **or otherwise communicating with**, any regulator or government agency… whether or not the communication is required by law, and no such communication is a breach of this agreement or grounds for set-off under clause 15". Security firms plausibly serve councils, ministries, courts and public venues — all "government agencies", and all "clients" under 11.2 if served in the lookback window. Read literally, a sales pitch to a council client is "otherwise communicating with a government agency": no breach, no set-off. This seam predates v6, but the new client-protection layer is exactly where it bites — it hands the Vendor a 14.4 answer to the no-contact rule and the 11.6 trigger for an entire client category. The fix must exclude only *qua-client commercial* contact and leave every genuine regulator communication untouched.

**Fix (one edit in clause 14.4).** After "whether or not the communication is required by law", insert:
> ; provided that this clause does not apply to communications with a government agency solely in its capacity as a client or prospective client of either Company or of any Restrained Business

**Regulatory-integrity check — examined closely, because this narrows 14.4's literal breadth:** every report, complaint, disclosure, submission and act of cooperation, and every communication with any body acting as regulator, prosecutor, licensing authority or investigator — required or voluntary — remains absolutely protected; the exclusion operates only where the agency is a *customer* being dealt with commercially, which is not a regulator communication in substance. The PSPLA→CIPU gateway is unaffected. **Holds.**

### N21 — [RECOMMENDED] — cl 11.4 — the CPP end date is hardcoded to 1 October 2026 while cl 1.1 lets the Settlement Date float
