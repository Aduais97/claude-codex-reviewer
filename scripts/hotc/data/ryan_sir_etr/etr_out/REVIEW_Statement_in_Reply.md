## A. Executive summary — the bottom line

The draft is, in structure and tone, a competent and largely sound pleading. Mr McGoldrick has the right shape: a clear denial, a clean separation of the two respondent entities, the correct central defence (employment began 12 March 2025, so pre-2025 matters are not SLS RTC's), a strong hours-reduction defence built on the signed s63A variation, and a good "paid-in-full" answer to the radio incident. **But it cannot be filed as it stands.** There are three classes of problem to close before 21 August, in this order of urgency:

1. **A wrong-year typo that directly contradicts your central defence.** In the summary/"further information" section the draft states your employee's start date as **"12 March 2026"** (line 133), where every other reference — correctly — says **12 March 2025**. Filed uncorrected, this is not a cosmetic slip: it makes your headline defence internally incoherent (it would place the start of employment *after* the very complaints and record requests he made in April–June 2026) and hands the applicant an easy point.

2. **Eight unresolved `[bracket]` gaps, including a literal `[insert date]`.** A pleading filed with open editorial brackets reads as careless to the Authority. More seriously, the brackets sit over your two most dangerous claims — the **failure to investigate the 14 June complaint** and the **records/Privacy request** — which means those claims are, right now, effectively *unanswered*. The brackets are not just drafting placeholders; they are the lawyer telling you *"I cannot plead this until you give me the facts and the emails."*

3. **An entity-name typo and a registry/address inconsistency.** The buyer entity is mis-typed **"SLC RTC Limited"** in the key transaction sentence (line 52); the Reply is addressed to the Authority **"at Auckland"** on what is a **Christchurch** file (line 32); and the address-for-service block pairs an **Auckland** street address with the **Christchurch (03)** phone number (line 150). Address-for-service errors are consequential — that is where every subsequent document gets served on you.

**Where you are most exposed, substantively:**

- **The failure to investigate the 14 June 2026 complaint is your single biggest liability, and the draft's own bracket concedes it.** The note at line 115 records that your employee says you emailed him on 14 July 2026 saying you *"assumed another manager had dealt with the complaint."* On the known facts, a formal complaint against a manager sat for a month with only an acknowledgement and no investigation. Critically, **this claim is NOT time-barred** (complaint 14 June, Statement of Problem 24 July — well inside 90 days), so the time-bar refrain that carries much of the draft does not save it. It must be defended on the merits, and at present it is not defended at all.

- **The records / Privacy Act supply is your second exposure, and the draft again concedes uncertainty** — the bracket `[When were records provided?]` (line 96) is you admitting you do not yet know whether you actually met his request. Pointing him to PayHero may not, on its own, discharge the wage-and-time-records obligation or the Privacy Act access request.

- **The draft over-relies on the 90-day time-bar.** Used correctly it defeats the hours-reduction and Christmas-party claims. But it is applied as a blanket to matters that are, or may be, *in time* — the complaint-handling claim certainly is, and some of the March 2026 "management conduct" incidents may be if your employee's 14 June complaint raised them. Leaning on a time-bar that does not reach these claims is a strategic weak point.

- **One statutory citation looks wrong.** The 90-day time-bar is pleaded as **"section 103(3)"** (line 76). On the face of the Act the 90-day raising rule is **section 114** (with s115 for exceptional circumstances). Please have Mr McGoldrick confirm — if it is wrong, it is the kind of error the other side will enjoy pointing out.

The good news: the two defences you most need — the **s63A hours variation** and the **radio-shift-was-paid** point — are well made and, provided you hold the signed variation and the pay record, should hold. The work between now and filing is (1) fix the errors, (2) fill the eight brackets with real facts and documents, and (3) get honest with your lawyer about the 14 June complaint so the denial you file is one you can actually stand behind.

### D3. Statutory citation for the 90-day time-bar looks wrong
**Location:** Line 76 (and the un-numbered "time-barred / no consent" refrains at lines 44, 82, 100, 134).
**Current text:**
> "Mr Webby cannot raise a personal grievance about the above matters due to **section 103(3) of the Act**. Even if he had a personal grievance (which is denied), he is time-barred from raising this grievance."

**Issue.** On the face of the Employment Relations Act 2000, the **90-day rule for *raising* a personal grievance is section 114** (an employee must raise the grievance within 90 days of the action, or of the date it came to their notice, whichever is later), with **section 115** governing the Authority's leave to raise it out of time in exceptional circumstances. Section **103** defines *what* a personal grievance is; it is not, on my reading, the source of the 90-day limit. "Section 103(3)" therefore looks like an incorrect citation.

**Action:** Ask Mr McGoldrick to confirm the correct provision. I flag this as *"please verify with counsel,"* but with reasonable confidence that the intended reference is **s114** (and s115). The *substance* of the defence — that these grievances are out of time and you do not consent to late raising — is correct and well made; it is only the section number that needs checking. A wrong statutory citation in an otherwise strong pleading is exactly the kind of thing opposing counsel will use to suggest carelessness.

**Location:** The repeated "time-barred / no consent to late raising" passages — lines 44, 76, 82, 100, 134 — read against the specific claims.

| Claim | Triggering event | In / out of time? | Comment |
|-------|------------------|-------------------|---------|
| Hours reduction | Variation effective 28 Sep 2025 | **Out of time** | Time-bar sound *and* he signed the variation (consent). Strong. |
| Christmas party pay | Nov/Dec 2025 | **Out of time** (if not raised earlier) | Time-bar sound — but confirm he didn't raise it in correspondence before the SOP. |

| Radio incident | 23 Jul 2026 | **In time** | Defended on merits (paid). Good. |

**Action:** Keep the time-bar where it works (hours, Christmas party). ### G4. Radio shift was paid — defeating that disadvantage claim
