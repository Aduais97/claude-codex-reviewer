## 3. SOUNDNESS OF THE KEY DEFENCES

1. **Employer identity / no liability before 12 March 2025 (lines 45–46, 52–60).** Conceptually sound *as a matter of corporate identity*: separate acquiring company; new agreement signed 9 Mar 2025; employment with the seller ended 11 Mar 2025; SLS RTC's Holidays Act obligations pleaded to run from 12 Mar 2025. **But the defence is now exposed on two fronts and must not be relied on as pleaded:** (a) **C1** (the "2026" typo, which on its face concedes the 2025 period), and (b) **Part 6A of the ERA (R7, corrected)** — because **security work IS a specified category under Schedule 1A**, the 11 March 2025 sale of the security business likely engaged Part 6A's continuity-of-employment protections. Unless the small-to-medium-enterprise exemption applies (incoming employer under 20 employees), Mr Webby's **service and accrued leave may be continuous across the transfer**, which would defeat the blanket "cannot respond to anything before 12 March 2025" stance. **Resolve C1 and R7 before relying on this defence.**
2. **s63A hours variation (lines 61–75).** Strong. A 4 Sep 2025 consultation email, a stated seven-day process, ~11-FTE rationale tied to client budget cuts, a signed 16 Sep 2025 variation, an effective date of 28 Sep 2025, and an express independent-advice entitlement together evidence a compliant s63A process (s63A does require, for a variation, that the employer give a copy of the intended agreement, advise of the right to independent advice, allow a reasonable opportunity to take it, and consider issues raised). **Holds provided the signed 16 September 2025 variation is produced.**
3. **Paid radio shift (lines 123–128).** Strong. Legitimate health-and-safety basis (uncharged radio → unable to communicate with the shift party), employee responsibility for equipment, sent home for safety, **and paid for the shift** — which defeats the "disadvantage" element. **Holds provided the 23 July 2026 payslip is produced** and the date wording is corrected (M5).
4. **Christmas party (lines 77–82).** Reasonable: voluntary attendance, gift cards as a gift rather than payment for attendance, and the claim is out of time. Sound.

**Net:** the defensive spine is good, but the employer-identity defence is now the weakest link, not the strongest — the risk is concentrated in (a) the two in-time claims that are currently unanswered (C3, C4), (b) the drafting/precision defects (C1, C2, C5, M1–M6), and (c) the **Part 6A continuity-of-service exposure (R7)**, which is a first-order legal question rather than a footnote.

## 4. STATUTORY-CITATION VERIFICATION

Citations below have been checked directly against the official Employment Relations Act 2000 as currently in force (New Zealand Legislation, latest reprint). Counsel to settle the final citations against the in-force text at the date of filing.

- **s63A (line 74)** — **correct** for the variation process. On a variation to an individual employment agreement, s63A requires the employer to provide a copy of the intended agreement, advise the employee of the entitlement to seek independent advice, give a reasonable opportunity to do so, and consider and respond to issues raised. Primary source: https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2000/24/en/latest/#DLM59152 **Keep.**
- **"s103(3)" (line 76)** — **incorrect** for the 90-day bar. Section 103 defines the *types* of personal grievance. The 90-day limitation is **s114** ("Raising personal grievance"); out-of-time leave (exceptional circumstances) is **s115** ("Further period for raising personal grievance"). Under s114 the grievance must be raised within 90 days of the action occurring or coming to the employee's notice, whichever is later (12 months for sexual-harassment grievances since the 2023 amendment); s115 allows the Authority to grant leave to raise out of time on exceptional-circumstances grounds. Primary sources: s114 — https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2000/24/en/latest/#DLM60354 ; s115 — https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2000/24/en/latest/#DLM60355 **Correct the citation to s114 (and s115 where an out-of-time argument is run).**

This directly bears on the employer-identity defence and must be addressed in the pleading strategy. Primary sources: Schedule 1A — https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2000/24/en/latest/#DLM61724 ; Part 6A — https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2000/24/en/latest/#DLM59172 ; 2021 security-officer extension — https://www.legislation.govt.nz/regulation/public/2021/0058/latest/whole.html

**R7 — Continuity of service / accrued leave from the 11 March 2025 business transfer — PART 6A OF THE ERA APPLIES TO SECURITY WORK (SUBSTANTIVE LEGAL RISK — CORRECTED).**

*Correction to the earlier draft of this review:* an earlier version of this memo stated that security guarding was **not** a specified category under Schedule 1A / Part 6A, and reasoned that Part 6A's continuity protections therefore did not apply — which was said to *help* the "new employment from 12 March 2025" position. **That was wrong.** **Security work IS a specified category.** "Services in the security sector" were inserted into **Schedule 1A** with effect from **1 July 2021** by the **Employment Relations (Extending Part 6A Protections to Security Officers) Order 2021 (LI 2021/58)**. Mr Webby — employed as a Covert Retail Loss Prevention Officer with the seller and a Retail Security Officer with SLS RTC — is on his face within that category (subject to counsel confirming the role meets the Schedule 1A definition of security work).

*Why this matters — the corrected analysis:*
- **Part 6A subpart 1 applies to a "restructuring," which expressly includes the sale or transfer of an employer's business (or part of it) to another person.** The 11 March 2025 purchase of SLS Retail Theft Control's **security business** by SLS RTC is, on its face, exactly such a restructuring.
- As a specified-category ("vulnerable") employee, Mr Webby had the **statutory right to elect to transfer** to SLS RTC on his **existing terms and conditions**, with his employment treated as **continuous**. If Part 6A engaged, his **service and accrued service-related entitlements — including accrued annual holidays under the Holidays Act 2003 — carry over**, and the First Respondent's pre-transfer liabilities may pass to, or be apportioned with, the Second Respondent.
- This **reverses** the earlier premise and **materially undercuts the pleading's central defence.** The Reply's blanket position that SLS RTC "was not the employer before 12 March 2025," that it "cannot respond to anything before that date," and that Holidays Act entitlements run **only** from 12 March 2025 (lines 46, 60, 88, 133), is likely **overstated** if Part 6A applied — because continuity would place SLS RTC in a continuous-service relationship reaching back to Mr Webby's employment with the seller (which Ryan dates to **20 November 2024**). This directly feeds the underpayment / leave-calculation claim (lines 84–97) and Ryan's records request from 2024 (lines 86, 90): if service is continuous, SLS RTC's refusal to engage with pre-12-March-2025 leave/records is exposed.
- Note that the offer of new employment "**on the same terms and conditions**" (line 53) is **consistent with** — not contrary to ��� a Part 6A transfer; it reinforces the continuity reading rather than defeating it.

*Defensive angles counsel should test (this is not all one-way):*
- **(a) SME exemption.** If SLS RTC (the **incoming** employer) employed **fewer than 20 employees** at 11 March 2025, it is **exempt** from Part 6A subpart 1's transfer obligations, which could be a complete answer to the point. The 4 September 2025 email's estimate of ~11 surplus full-time roles hints the workforce may have exceeded that, but headcount at the transfer date is unknown — **counsel to confirm.**
- **(b) Whether a Part 6A election in fact occurred**, or the parties genuinely structured a fresh start. Part 6A protections for specified employees cannot simply be contracted out of, so the *manner* of the transaction and the terms of the 9 March 2025 individual agreement matter.
- **(c) Apportionment.** Even if service is continuous, the Act provides for accrued service-related entitlements to be apportioned between the outgoing (First Respondent) and incoming (Second Respondent) employers; the sale agreement may allocate the pre-transfer liability to the First Respondent.

**Action:** counsel to (1) confirm SLS RTC's employee headcount at 11 March 2025 (SME exemption); (2) confirm whether the transaction was run as a Part 6A transfer and what election, if any, was offered; (3) review the sale/transfer agreement and the 9 March 2025 individual agreement for how continuity of service and accrued leave were treated and apportioned; and (4) **recalibrate the "no liability / cannot respond before 12 March 2025" stance accordingly.** Do **not** file the blanket clean-break position without resolving this: if Part 6A applied and the SME exemption does not, that stance is wrong in law and gives ground on the in-time underpayment/leave claim.
