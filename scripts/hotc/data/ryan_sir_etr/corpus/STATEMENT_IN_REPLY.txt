The Second Respondent’s account of the relevant facts is:
Background to Employment 
The Second Respondent, SLS RTC Limited, is a limited liability company that operates a specialised retail security and loss prevention business, focusing on covert guarding, electronic gates, and surveillance. 
On 11 March 2025, SLC RTC Limited completed its purchase of the security business of SLS Retail Theft Control Limited, the First Respondent. 
Prior to the completion of this purchase, SLS RTC Limited arranged with SLS Retail Theft Control Limited that it would offer new employment to SLS Retail Theft Control Limited’s employees (including Mr Webby) on the same terms and conditions as they had been employed with SLS Retail Theft Control Limited. 
Mr Webby had been employed by SLS Retail Theft Control Limited as a Covert Retail Loss Prevention Officer. 
On 9 March 2025, Mr Webby accepted SLS RTC Limited’s offer of new employment and signed a new individual employment agreement for the role of Retail Security Officer.  The employment agreement (which is relied upon as if pleaded in full) outlined that: 
The role was for a 40-hour working week; 
Employment would commence on 12 March 2025; and 
No travel time would be paid while working in the metropolitan Christchurch area. 
On 11 March 2025, Mr Webby’s employment with SLS Retail Theft Control Limited ended.
On 12 March 2025, Mr Webby’s employment with SLS RTC Limited commenced.  This is the date from which SLS RTC Limited began providing Mr Webby’s entitlements under the Holidays Act 2003. 
Agreed Reduction in Working Hours
On 4 September 2025, SLS RTC Limited’s director, Ahmad Duais, sent an email to employees outlining that: 
Over the prior few weeks, there had been significant reductions in client budgets resulting in reduced available work and hours for its employees; 
It was commencing a seven-day consultation process to determine how to best address staffing levels in light of the reduced available work; 
It estimated the reduction in hours could mean that SLS RTC Limited had a surplus of approximately 11 full-time roles for its business needs;
If roles were confirmed to be redundant, it would use an objective scoring matrix to identify to determine which employees would be offered the remaining roles; and
It considered that one option under consultation would be to vary employment agreements to reduce minimum weekly hours to 30 hours, but that employees were under no obligation to accept reduced hours. 
SLS RTC Limited invited feedback on its proposal for change and reaffirmed that it was only consulting and no final decisions had been made.
On 16 September 2025, SLS RTC Limited and Mr Webby entered into a formal variation to Mr Webby’s employment agreement. 
The variation specified that: 
The changes to Mr Webby's employment agreement would be effective from 28 September 2025; 
The clause specifying Mr Webby’s minimum weekly hours would be varied from 40 hours to 35 hours per week; and
Mr Webby was entitled to seek independent advice about the variation prior to signing.
SLS RTC Limited complied with section 63A of the Act when varying Mr Webby’s employment agreement.  
SLS RTC Limited therefore denies that it breached Mr Webby’s employment agreement when he was subsequently rostered to work a minimum of 35 hours per week.
Mr Webby first complained about this reduction in hours to SLS RTC Limited on 14 July 2026.  Mr Webby cannot raise a personal grievance about the above matters due to section 103(3) of the Act.  Even if he had a personal grievance (which is denied), he is time-barred from raising this grievance.  SLS RTC Limited does not consent to the late raising of any grievance.
Christmas Party 2025
In or about late November or early December 2025, SLS RTC Limited hosted a company Christmas party.  
Attendance at this party was voluntary but it was hosted in order to provide an opportunity for staff to relax and celebrate the end of the year.  Gift cards were provided to staff members as a gift and not as any form of payment for attendance at the Christmas party. 
Mr Webby asserted to Mr Duais that his attendance at the Christmas party was mandatory and sought payment for his attendance. 
Mr Duais responded to Mr Webby to outline that attendance at the Christmas party was not mandatory and that there would be no reimbursement for time attending as money had already been spent by SLS RTC Limited to host the event as a way to thank Mr Webby and his colleagues for their hard work during the year. 
Mr Webby did not raise a personal grievance in relation to this claim until his Statement of Problem filed 24 July 2026.  Accordingly, he is time-barred from raising this grievance.  SLS RTC Limited does not consent to the late raising of any grievance.
