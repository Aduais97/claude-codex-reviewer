## 0. Scope, standard, and how the available corpus is used

**Engagement type & standard.** This is a buy-side acquisition-diligence report on a precinct-patrol security-services business ("the Target"), executed to Big Four (Deloitte-standard) transaction-diligence conventions. It is the analytical spine of the deal — target overview, investment thesis, workstreams, valuation method, risk register, strategic-fit lens, benchmarking, key findings and a decision-oriented conclusion — not a completed valuation.

**The corpus, and what it is not.** The evidence supplied is an operational dataset only: `SOURCE_DATA.txt` (a monthly time series of patrol/business calls and incidents, March 2024 – July 2026, with a March–July 2026 incident-category breakdown) and `REPORT.md` (a Heart of the City (HOTC) precinct quarterly safety report built from that data). The corpus contains **no profit-and-loss statement, balance sheet, cash flow, org chart, delegated-authority matrix, contract register, client list, payroll, licence schedule, or asking price.** Consequently the financial, valuation, and organisational chapters are executed as **method + explicit data request + confidence grade**, never as fabricated numbers. Inventing a P&L or an EBITDA bridge from a patrol-activity file would be the single largest diligence-integrity failure available in this engagement.

**What one programme means for scope (central framing).** The corpus evidences **exactly one programme — the HOTC precinct-safety contract, a Business Improvement District (BID)-type arrangement.** It does **not** evidence the Target's *full* contract base, client list, service-line mix, or ownership of its revenue streams. This report therefore:
- treats the HOTC programme as **the single revenue relationship that is evidenced**, and analyses it to Confidence A/B; and
- treats every statement about the Target's *overall* client concentration, BID dependency, or single-contract status as a **Confidence-C hypothesis to be tested** against the §12 data pack (D-03 contract register, D-04 client-level revenue, D-14 grant/funding agreements) — **not** as an established finding.

The distinction is deliberate and material: *only one programme is visible in the supplied data* is evidenced; *the Target has only one programme* is **not** — the corpus simply cannot see the rest of the book. Where the downside case (near-total concentration) would dominate valuation and structure, that is stated as a **conditional** driven by D-03/D-04/D-14, and no concentration conclusion is finalised until they clear.

**What the corpus *does* support is fully exploited.** The operational time series is real, granular, and directly relevant to a security target's demand profile, seasonality, service mix, severity trend and productivity. Workstream E (§8) executes seasonality, mix-shift, severity-trend, funding/volume-elasticity, and pilot-decomposition analysis on the actual figures to Confidence A/B, and feeds the evidenced run-rates into valuation and benchmarking. Every number there is recomputed from source and carries its own data-quality caveat.

**Confidence grading (applied throughout).** **A** — recomputed from supplied source data. **B** — supported by source but with a stated estimation or window limitation. **C** — method defined; requires management information not yet supplied (every C item appears in the §12 data-request pack with a materiality rating).

**Integrity commitment.** No P&L, EBITDA, revenue-per-FTE, headcount, licence status, or valuation multiple is asserted for this Target. Governance quality, ownership structure and key-person dependency are **not characterised beyond what the data evidences** (which is: nothing — see §5.1–5.3). Every quantitative claim below is either recomputed from `SOURCE_DATA.txt` (A/B) or routed to §12 (C). That boundary is the deliverable.

### 1.2 Investment thesis (provisional — the equity story to be tested)

The plausible reason to buy *this* target *now* is **buy-and-build consolidation of recurring, essential-service precinct-patrol revenue.** On the single evidenced programme, that revenue is anchored by a Business Improvement District (BID)-type counterparty (HOTC). The thesis to test is acquisition of: (a) a contracted, demand-backed revenue base in a defined CBD geography; (b) a workforce and dispatch capability that can be route-density-blended with an acquirer's existing patrol footprint; and (c) an evidenced, **positive** capacity-to-output relationship — funding and safety output move firmly in the same direction, at a broadly comparable unit cost (§8.6) — that makes incremental funded coverage predictable to price and staff. The three value drivers to confirm: **route-density / guard-utilisation synergy, public-sector/BID revenue access, and a recurring contracted base.** None of the three can be valued until the §12 financial and contract pack is delivered, and the *breadth* of the revenue base (one contract or many) is itself unknown (§4.2, D-03/D-04).

Until D-06 clears, the buyer cannot confirm the acquired entity can lawfully operate the day after completion.

Because the downside case is severe and currently unfalsifiable, this is carried at **Confidence C** and D-03/D-04/D-14 are **Critical** (§4.2, §9 R5/R6).

- **H&S trend (R4).** Rising serious incidents (4 → 10 → 16 across the quarter), on an already-elevated pre-pilot incident density, against an assault-exposed, potentially lone-worker workforce.

## 4. Workstream A — Commercial & market diligence

**4.1 Contract base (C).** No contract register was supplied. Required per counterparty: service, annual value, term, renewal/retender date, termination and price-review clauses, exclusivity, SLAs and penalty exposure — **and, critically, the total number of contracts and clients** (the corpus shows only the HOTC programme). Routed to D-03.

**4.2 Revenue durability & concentration *(Confidence C — hypothesis to test; demand-side evidence from §8 is A)*.** The commercial crux is client concentration, and it **cannot be resolved from the corpus.** The only revenue relationship *visible in the supplied data* is the single BID-type HOTC precinct programme, whose funder has already exercised a **~14% budget cut** once (April/May 2025). **This does not establish that HOTC is the Target's only contract** — no client list or contract register was supplied, so the wider book is simply invisible here. The analysis is therefore framed conditionally:
- **If** the HOTC programme is the Target's primary/only revenue source, concentration and retender risk are **near-total**, the renewal calendar for the anchor contract is the single most important unknown in the deal, and the valuation must carry a large concentration discount (§7).
- **If** the Target holds a diversified contract book, concentration risk is materially lower and the HOTC budget cut is a single-client datapoint rather than an existential one.

Only **D-03 (contract register)** and **D-04 (client-level revenue & margin)** can distinguish these, and until they do, **no concentration conclusion is finalised.** What *is* evidenced (Confidence A) is that the *demand* underlying the HOTC programme is substantial, recurring and durable in shape (§8.2) — the open question is the **contractual security and breadth** of the revenue, not whether the work exists.

**4.3 Pipeline & win/loss (C).** No tender pipeline or win-rate history supplied. Routed to D-03/D-04.

**4.4 Demand drivers (A on realised demand).** The corpus gives a **direct read on realised demand** rather than proxies: a multi-year monthly series of calls and, from March 2026, categorised incidents. Realised demand on the HOTC programme is (i) **substantial and recurring** (~178–265 calls/month post-cut), (ii) **seasonal** (October and December peaks recur), and (iii) **capacity-elastic** — when a team was added, recorded demand served rose immediately (§8.5), which is consistent with latent, unmet demand at the current funding level (see honesty caveat, §5-MI F-6).

**4.5 Pricing (C).** No rate card, guard-hour pricing, or indexation terms supplied. The only unit-price signals are the operational cost-per-output anchors in §8.6, which are *funder cost per output*, not the target's *sell rate or margin*. Routed to D-04/D-05.

Critical to both org analysis and unit economics; routed to D-05. **Sector context (Confidence C, qualitative — no numeric norm is asserted, because the supplied pack contains no peer benchmark data):** manned-guarding attrition is characteristically high, licensed-guard supply is structurally tight, and penal-rate and minimum-wage exposure is material to gross margin. ### 5.7 Health & safety governance *(A signal + C)*

The fuller detailed-window series (March–July 2026) is **13 → 9 → 4 → 10 → 16**: not a clean monotonic climb (March opened at 13), so the honest read is a **sharp trough-to-peak escalation over the most recent three months, with July the window high**, rather than a straight-line trend. HSWA duty-holder compliance, WorkSafe history, critical-risk controls and lone-worker protocols require D-07; but the trend alone justifies H&S as a **High** risk (R4) now.

#### 5-MI.b Findings list — corrections with severity, exact text, and replacement

**F-1 — CRITICAL — "Near-linear / essentially the same rate" overstates the ROI relationship (average vs marginal conflation).**
The report compares the **average** cost per call on core funding (~$276 = total funding ÷ total calls) with the **marginal** cost per *extra* call on the pilot (~$300 = extra cost ÷ extra output) and calls them "essentially the same … a predictable, near-linear rate." These are **different measures**: the marginal rate sits **~9% above** the average, which indicates **mild diminishing returns, not linearity**. It also contradicts the report's own elasticity figures (a −14% funding cut associated with a −21% output fall implies an elasticity of ~1.5, i.e. *super*-proportional on the downside, while the marginal-vs-average gap implies *sub*-proportional on the upside — the two cannot both be "linear"). This is the report's central ROI claim and the most likely to be challenged by a numerate board or Council reviewer.
- **Current:** *"Pilot delivered extra output at ~$300 per extra call/incident — essentially the same rate as the core (~$276/call). Funding converts to output at a predictable, near-linear rate."*
- **Replacement:** *"The pilot delivered additional output at ~$300 per extra business call — a marginal rate close to, and about 9% above, the core programme's ~$276 average cost per call. Read with the post-cut step-down, this shows funding and safety output move firmly in the same direction, with extra funding buying extra output at a broadly comparable unit cost (slightly higher at the margin, consistent with mild diminishing returns rather than a fixed linear rate)."*
- *Why it still helps the case:* the honest version remains a strong, favourable message — additional funding buys additional output at close to the core rate — without the indefensible "linear" claim.

**F-2 — MAJOR — "Roughly doubled incidents in June" is false at the total level.**
Total incidents rose from 169 (May) to 271 (June) = **+60%, not ~100%**. The doubling occurred in two *sub-categories* — aggressive-behaviour (95 vs ~50 baseline) and theft (58 vs 31) — not in total incidents.
- **Current:** *"lifted calls +52% and roughly doubled incidents in June"*
- **Replacement:** *"lifted June calls by ~47% versus May (265 vs 180) and drove a ~60% rise in incidents (271 vs 169), with the two largest categories — aggressive-behaviour and theft records — roughly doubling."*

### 8.1 Reconciliation & MI integrity (A/B)

Quarter totals reproduce **exactly**: calls **180 + 265 + 228 = 673**; incidents **169 + 271 + 227 = 667**; monthly category sums tie (May 169, June 271, July 227). MI is arithmetically sound. **Five caveats are carried forward:**

3. **Five-month detail window.** Full incident-category detail exists **only from March 2026**, so any "record/all-time" incident claim is bounded to that five-month window (see F-4).

## 13. Decision framework & conclusion

### 13.1 Go / no-go gates (sequential)

1. **R1/D-06 — licensing transferable on change of control?** If no → **no-go** (or restructure to an asset/licence-contingent deal).
2. **Maintainable EBITDA (ex-pilot/grant) supportable at the thesis?** If no → renegotiate or walk. (§8.5 warns the pilot uplift is non-perpetuating.)
3. **Client concentration and anchor-contract renewal risk (R5/R6, D-03/D-04) acceptable or earn-out-mitigable?** First **establish** the concentration position (it is currently unknown, §4.2); if it proves high and the anchor-renewal risk is unacceptable → structure or walk.
4. **H&S / claims / holiday-pay exposures quantifiable and capped?** If no → indemnity/escrow or walk. (Rising serious incidents and elevated pre-pilot density, §8.4, sharpen this.)

### 13.2 Recommended posture

**Proceed to confirmatory diligence; do not issue an indicative price.** When D-01/D-02 arrive, strike a range via §7's mechanism, weighted to **deferred/earn-out** consideration on (a) contract renewal and (b) normalised-EBITDA delivery, with **escrow** against R1/R4/R11 and **retention lock-ins** against R8. Price chips already identified: **pilot/grant revenue excluded from run-rate (§8.5), leave-liability remediation (R11), and an MI-reliability adjustment (§5-MI).**

### 13.3 Conclusion

**The operational asset is real and its demand base is evidenced (§2.5, §8) — but the financial, organisational, and legal core of the acquisition case, and the *breadth* of its revenue base, are not yet substantiated by the supplied corpus, and no valuation or fair-value recommendation can responsibly be issued until the §12 pack is delivered.** The activity data are granular, reconcile to source, and reveal a genuine, capacity-elastic demand base with a demonstrable marginal-productivity anchor. The target's own reporting is arithmetically sound but its value narrative over-reaches in five identifiable places — one materially (the "near-linear" ROI claim) — which is itself a diligence finding (R10) and a price-chip. The things that will decide this deal — **licence transferability, maintainable ex-pilot EBITDA, the client-concentration position, and anchor-contract renewal** — are all currently unknown. Critically, **the report does not conclude that the target is a single-contract business**: the corpus shows only one programme, which is not the same as the target having only one, and that distinction is held open until D-03/D-04 resolve it. The disciplined position is to **fund the data request, hold the price, and refuse to manufacture the numbers — or the concentration verdict — the corpus does not contain.**
