## Executive Summary

**This engagement cannot yet produce a fair-value opinion, an organisational-efficiency verdict, or a valuation — because the data required to do so does not exist in the file provided.** That is the single most important finding in this report, and stating it plainly is the correct professional outcome, not a deficiency in the analysis.

The instruction set for this engagement asks for a forensic organisational review (map every employee, revenue per FTE, GP per FTE, headcount-restructuring scenarios), a normalised-earnings and EBITDA bridge, validation of the seller's profit claims, and a multi-method valuation against an asking price. Each of those deliverables requires source data — payroll and org charts, a profit-and-loss account, an adjustments schedule, a data-room, an asking price. **None of that data is present in the corpus.** The corpus is a single operating KPI: business-call counts and recorded patrol-days for two fiscal years. There is no headcount, no revenue line, no cost line, no contract, no licence register, and no price.

To produce revenue-per-FTE ratios, an EBITDA bridge, or an EV/EBITDA range from this corpus would require inventing the underlying numbers. **We will not do that.** Fabricating an unsupported statistic is precisely the defect that caused a prior Absolute Security Group (ASG) document to be queried by Heart of the City (HOTC). The approved framework is explicit on this point: *"no conclusion outruns its evidence… No [U] item may enter a headline, valuation input, or IC recommendation"* and *"absent workstreams must appear as explicit data-sufficiency gaps, not silent omissions."* This report therefore does two things, and only two things:

1. **Executes, in full, the one workstream the data supports** — independent verification and forensic trend analysis of the KPI (framework §9–§12). Here the analysis is complete, reproducible, and defensible to the last digit.
2. **Converts every unsupported workstream into a precise, priced data request** (framework §2–§8, §13–§16), so the Investment Committee sees exactly what must be obtained before a valuation or an organisational verdict can be issued — and exactly what each dataset would let us compute.

### Bottom line for the Investment Committee

> **NOT READY TO PROCEED to a binding offer on current data.** Recommendation: **GO to next-phase (confirmatory) diligence**, conditional on delivery of the data-request list in §1. No purchase price should be discussed, and no organisational-headcount decision taken, until the conditions precedent below are met.

### Deal merits established from the data — evidence grade [S]

- **Sustained, material CBD business engagement.** The service generated **2,505 recorded business calls in FY26** and **3,042 in FY25** — a durable ~2.4k–3.0k annual demand signal from CBD businesses. Demand for the service is real and repeat.
- **FY26 holds two of the three strongest recent months.** Across the 22-month like-for-like comparison window (Aug–Jun, both years), **December FY26 (305) ranks #1 and October FY26 (302) ranks #3**; FY25 January (303) sits between them at #2. The service is still capable of its best-ever months, and produced two of them in the most recent year.
- **A strong second quarter and a strong June close.** **June closed +39% year-on-year (265 vs 191)** — the largest positive monthly swing in either year — and the **October–December quarter beat the prior year by +12.6%** (832 vs 739). *(These are the two positive periods within FY26; on their own they do not establish an improving trend — H2 as a whole fell −21%; see §9.4.)*

### Critical red flags — evidence grade [S]

- **The standalone dataset cannot support a value case on its own.** Financials, contracts, licences, and workforce data are entirely absent. Every valuation, commercial, and organisational conclusion is a condition precedent, not a priced item.
- **The like-for-like trend is negative on volume and worse on productivity.** Business calls fell **−10.6% like-for-like (Aug–Jun: 2,419 vs 2,707)**, and on a per-recorded-day basis the fall is **larger, at −14.9% (6.76 vs 7.94 calls/day)** — because FY26 used *more* recorded days to produce *fewer* calls. *(Per-day metrics are computed on "recorded days," a logged operational proxy whose formal definition is not in the corpus — see §11 and R15; the like-for-like comparison applies it consistently across both years.)*
- **The report's own explanation for the decline is contradicted by the only evidence in the file.** REPORT.md attributes the decline to a *"reduced coverage footprint,"* asserting *"FY25 operated a larger patrol-coverage programme."* The sole coverage proxy in the corpus — recorded patrol-days — shows the two years are **essentially equal (FY26 374 vs FY25 376)** and that FY26 was actually **higher** on the like-for-like window (**358 vs 341**). The coverage-reduction narrative is **unsupported and, on the available proxy, contradicted.** This is the highest-risk line in the client report and is addressed in detail in §10 and the correction schedule (Appendix A).

### The one instruction we are declining, and why

The engagement brief asserts, as background, that *"the calls are down ~11% year-on-year because FY26 runs reduced patrol coverage vs a larger FY25 budget."* **That causal claim cannot be verified from the corpus, and the one relevant proxy in the corpus points the other way.** We flag it rather than adopt it. If ASG holds patrol-hours, roster, or coverage-budget figures that substantiate the reduced-coverage story, supplying even one of them resolves the issue immediately (see §10). Until then, the honest statement is narrower: *call volume fell; the cause is not established by this data.*

### 9.2 Like-for-like per-recorded-day — basis-matched to the volume headline

Two per-day figures exist, on two different periods; both must be stated with their basis:

| Basis | FY26 | FY25 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full year (incl. part-reported July) | 6.70 /day | 8.09 /day | **−17.2%** |
| Like-for-like (Aug–Jun only) | 6.76 /day | 7.94 /day | **−14.9%** |

**Key observation:** the per-day like-for-like decline (**−14.9%**) is *larger* than the volume decline (**−10.6%**). That is arithmetically significant — it means FY26 consumed **more** recorded patrol-days to produce **fewer** calls. Output per unit of recorded coverage fell, and fell by more than headline volume did.

> **Denominator caveat — read with every per-day figure below.** "Recorded days" is a **logged operational proxy, not a defined metric.** No data dictionary in the corpus states what a "recorded day" is, and several weekly buckets are logged at **8 days** — a non-calendar period (see §11). The like-for-like *comparison* is valid to the extent the proxy is applied consistently across both years (a single consistent source supports that), but the **interpretation of the per-day change as "productivity" is provisional [P]** until the metric definition is supplied (§11, R15). We therefore present the per-day decline as a robust *arithmetic* fact and a *provisional* efficiency read — not a settled productivity conclusion.

### 9.3 Volume / coverage / productivity decomposition of the −288-call like-for-like gap

The Aug–Jun call gap is **2,419 − 2,707 = −288 calls**. Decomposing it into a coverage (recorded-days) effect and a productivity (per-day rate) effect:

| Effect | Computation | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Coverage** (recorded-days) | (358 − 341 days) × 7.94 FY25 rate | **+135 calls** |
| **Productivity** (per-day rate) | 358 days × (6.76 − 7.94) | **−423 calls** |
| **Net** | +135 − 423 | **−288 calls** ✅ (reconciles exactly) |

**Interpretation [S] on the arithmetic; [P] on the label:** the decomposition reconciles exactly, and the **coverage proxy did not shrink** over Aug–Jun — it *grew* (by 17 days), which on its own would have *added* ~135 calls. On that arithmetic, the like-for-like decline cannot be explained by *fewer* recorded days; it is a fall in calls **per recorded day**. This **quantitatively contradicts a "reduced coverage footprint" explanation** (developed further in §10). The caveat of §9.2 carries: because "recorded days" is an undefined proxy, whether the per-day fall is genuine *operational productivity loss* (versus a demand-side softening, a definition change, or a logging-behaviour shift) **cannot be resolved from the corpus** and is carried as R14/R15 pending deployment and metric-definition data (§5, §11).

## §10. Coverage-Footprint Evidence Test — the report's central attribution fails on the data

**Objective:** convert REPORT.md's "larger patrol-coverage programme" claim into a formal, auditable evidence test.

REPORT.md states, as fact: *"FY25 operated a larger patrol-coverage programme than FY26; the year-on-year movement primarily reflects the reduced coverage footprint, not reduced business engagement per patrol delivered."* We tested each embedded hypothesis against the corpus:

| Coverage hypothesis | Only observable proxy in corpus | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "FY25 ran a larger programme" (full year) | Recorded days **374 (FY26) vs 376 (FY25)** — essentially equal | **CONTRADICTED as "larger"; at most flat** |
| "FY25 larger" (like-for-like Aug–Jun) | Recorded days **358 (FY26) vs 341 (FY25)** — FY26 higher | **CONTRADICTED; FY26 coverage-days greater** |
| "Decline reflects reduced footprint, not engagement" | Decomposition (§9.3): coverage +135, productivity −423 | **CONTRADICTED by the data's own arithmetic** |
| "Engagement per patrol held up" | **No patrol count exists** | **UNTESTABLE [U]** — computation impossible |

**Conclusion:** the report's single interpretive sentence is its most exposed line. It asserts a cause (reduced coverage) that the only relevant proxy in the file **contradicts**, and it asserts a per-patrol efficiency conclusion that is **not computable** (there is no patrol count). This is exactly the class of unsupported, recomputation-inviting statement that triggered HOTC's prior query. Note that "recorded days" is itself a **non-standard, undefined proxy** (§11, R15); it is used here only to test the target's own "larger programme" claim on a like-for-like basis, and **even on that generous proxy the claim is not supported** — a stronger deployment measure is needed to draw *any* coverage conclusion, in either direction.

**Gate rule.** Any coverage/footprint attribution requires at least **one** of the following before it may be stated:

- patrol **hours** (budgeted vs delivered), or
- **route count** per week, or
- **FTE roster** by week, or
- a **coverage budget** figure ($ or shifts) for each year.

Until one exists, the only permitted statement is factual: *"FY26 was delivered under a changed patrol-coverage footprint relative to FY25; underlying deployment data is available on request."* The per-patrol efficiency claim must be **deleted, not softened** — see Appendix A, Finding F1 (CRITICAL). This is not merely a client-report wording issue: for the acquirer it means the target's own narrative for its most important operating trend is currently **unsubstantiated**, which is itself a data-governance finding (§11, R13).
