# Enhanced CBD Safety Team — LCF Pilot: Accountability & Value Report

## 1.  Executive Summary
The Enhanced CBD Safety Team pilot runs from 2 June to 24 July 2026 under the Local Crime Fund allocation administered by Heart of the City. This report presents verified activity data for 1 June – 18 July 2026 — 48 days of daily patrol logs, including the 1 June mobilisation day immediately preceding pilot commencement; the final pilot days (19–24 July) will be appended for the administrators’ report. ASG deployed two additional afternoon teams Sunday to Thursday, one enlarged combined team on Friday and Saturday, and a flexed evening uplift on peak nights — alongside the existing 24/7 patrol programme. This pilot delivers a targeted CPTED-based intervention, increasing visible guardianship, improving natural surveillance, and strengthening business-community safety networks during peak risk periods.

### Headline results
- **406 calls and incidents attended** in 48 days — 59.2 per week against a pre-uplift baseline of 40.7 (**+45%**) and 46.2 in the same weeks last year (**+28%**).
- **4,354 welfare check-ins with named precinct businesses** (≈90 per day) plus 678 block-level welfare sweeps — visible guardianship delivered street by street, every day of the pilot.
- **156 move-ons**, **142 aggression or disorder events attended and managed**, **79 theft responses** with repeated recoveries returned to stores, **35 welfare interventions** with vulnerable people, and **30 trespasses** actioned.
- **Unattended demand fell to near zero:** ASG’s pre-pilot capacity analysis (Jan–Apr 2026) recorded 211 missed interventions — 29% of all calls. Across the pilot to date (48 logged days) the logs show **three** capacity-affected responses (≈1%), and none in the two-team daytime window after the first week.
- **Police escalations stayed low: 39 across the pilot** (5.7/week vs 6.5 in the same weeks last year — -12%), while 13 arrests were directly supported and evidential incidents were posted to the Auror pipeline — 48 Auror reports are referenced in the pilot logs.
The pattern is consistent: when capacity was added, attended demand rose immediately and stayed high — while the share passed on to Police fell. The precinct’s demand for a responsive safety presence was always there; before this uplift, close to a third of it was going unmet.

The funded pool of approximately 1,115 service hours ($41,150 + GST) is fully allocated across the pilot roster to 24 July, and was invoiced in two equal instalments before 30 June 2026, per the funding agreement. Hours delivered to 18 July are evidenced by the daily patrol logs and the weekly data reports, which reconcile line-for-line with this report.
The two-team sector split — one team wrapping the upper CBD while the second covers the lower CBD — runs through the daily logs and shows up directly in outcomes: cross-team dispatches resolving calls within minutes, simultaneous incidents held at two locations at once, and coverage maintained during meetings, statements and prolonged incidents.

Rising attended-call numbers during a deterrence pilot are the capacity effect, not a safety deterioration: businesses call when a team reliably arrives. The same applies to category counts such as aggressive behaviour: these are attended and recorded events, which rise with patrol density and reporting confidence, not a measure of underlying prevalence. The team absorbed roughly half as much again in weekly demand while police escalation rates fell year-on-year — more incidents resolved at the guardianship tier, exactly where a CPTED intervention should resolve them. As a share of attended demand, police escalations fell against both comparators — 9.6% of attended calls during the pilot, against 10.8% in the baseline period and 14.1% in June–July 2025.

## 4.  Capacity — Unattended Demand, Before and During
The clearest single measure of the uplift is what happened to calls ASG could not attend.

| Period | Coverage model | Unattended / capacity-affected demand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jan – Apr 2026 (pre-uplift) | Single patrol team | 211 missed interventions — 29% of all calls; 51 “team busy” instances; 20 simultaneous-incident clashes |
| 1 Jun – 18 Jul 2026 (pilot to date) | Two teams Sun–Thu; enlarged team Fri–Sat | Three capacity-affected responses across 48 logged days (≈1% of demand) — all in the mobilisation/first week or on single-team night cover |
- 1 June — a Coffee Club caller was queued behind another incident on the morning the uplift was still standing up.
- 6 June — one cafe call could not be attended while guards managed an active four-person altercation.
- 2 July, 01:34 — the night team took two near-simultaneous calls and attended them back-to-back; the second caller waited minutes, and both were resolved.
In the two-team daytime window itself, unattended demand after the first week was **zero**. The logs show the opposite failure mode disappearing: cross-team dispatches (13–16 July), the team splitting to hold live response cover during the Auror meeting (17 July), and both teams converging on the same offender when needed (23 June). The residual pressure sits exactly where the uplift did not reach — single-team night cover — which indicates where any future capacity would have the greatest effect.

## 5.  Where the Capacity Went

[Chart: pilot totals by category]

### Theft response in action
The logs show a consistent interception pattern: staff call or flag a person of interest, the nearest team is dispatched — often the second team, already positioned in-sector — the POI is located within minutes, goods are recovered where possible, and the event is posted to Auror with descriptions and footage so repeat offenders accumulate a prosecutable file. Recoveries during the pilot included $1,083.91 of goods in a single multi-offender operation (23 June), $368 returned within five minutes (5 July), and a $1,377 theft with the offender identified and trespass requested (9 July).

## 6.  Business Engagement & Guardianship
- **4,354 welfare check-ins with named businesses** across the pilot — ≈90 named-store check-ins per day (individual stores may be checked more than once), plus 678 block-level sweeps.
- Face-to-face introductions and HOTC calling cards delivered across the precinct, including onboarding new stores to the service, with a clear message to stores: one number to call, and a team that responds.
- Store-level intelligence actively gathered and circulated — repeat-offender descriptions and photos collected from affected stores, shared between teams, and posted to Auror.
- Reporting behaviour coached — where thefts went unreported, guards followed up and walked staff through contacting HOTC immediately, converting passive victims into active reporters.
- Business feedback in the logs is direct: store teams sought guards out to thank them after incidents, and stores that had never engaged began calling the service during the pilot.

## 7.  Partnership & Collaboration
- **NZ Police** — 13 arrests directly supported during the pilot: offenders detained or tracked until Police arrival, statements given, scenes preserved, footage handed over. Escalation was orderly handover, not emergency callout.
- **Auckland Council** — joint handling of welfare and compliance matters: wardens engaged on trespass requests, a vulnerable young woman connected to Council support services for housing and financial help, and combined responses to street disorder.
- **Auror** — theft and repeat-offender incidents with evidential material (descriptions, footage, recoveries) are posted to Auror, building prosecutable files on repeat offenders; 48 Auror reports are referenced in the pilot logs. ASG met with Auror’s global headquarters on 17 July to deepen the integration, while keeping live response cover on the street.
- **Fire and Emergency** — bin fires attended and contained pending arrival (25 June, 14 July).
- **Venue and static security** — coordinated interventions with nightclub, hotel and site guards, including holding the Viaduct overnight after an assault on a static guard (14 July).

## 8.  Perceptions of Safety
The programme’s perceptions survey instrument (business, landlord and public-intercept forms) was prepared at pilot commencement. No survey wave has yet been completed, so no survey results are reported here. ASG recommends running the survey immediately post-pilot so that measured perceptions are referenced to the deployment period. In the interim, the qualitative record from daily engagement is consistent: staff thanking teams by name after incidents, businesses adopting HOTC-first reporting, new stores requesting onboarding, and repeat callers whose confidence in a response has visibly changed their reporting behaviour.

## 9.  Reduction in Harm & Community Wellbeing
The team operates welfare-first. Most street-community interactions in the logs end in voluntary compliance, a welfare conversation, or connection to support — not force. The period’s logs record no injuries to members of the public arising from any use of force by the team. Two injuries appear in the weekly data reports for the period — an offender assaulting a guard during the 8 July Mountain Warehouse theft recovery, and the 14 July assault on a venue static guard at the Viaduct — neither involving force used by the Safety Team. Aggression events were consistently de-escalated through presence, patience and negotiation.
- Vulnerable people escorted to safety and warmth, kept company while awaiting transport, connected with shelter information — including a woman sheltering from the cold escorted to a safe, warm location (5 July).
- Alcohol removed from minors and intoxicated persons in public spaces (10 logged public-drinking and liquor-ban interventions), and 5 medical-support events including seizure assistance and an unresponsive woman safeguarded until ambulance arrival (3 July).
- ASG-sponsored hot meals distributed overnight to rough sleepers on Queen Street (21 June) — the street-community trust this builds is the reason move-ons resolve without force.
- A switchblade taken off a 10-year-old after a violent threat, held safely until Police custody (4 July).

## 12.  Beyond the Pilot
The pilot set out to identify peak-risk windows, test visible-deterrence impact, and build a scalable model for the post-CRL environment. It has done all three. Demand concentrates exactly where the uplift was aimed — afternoon trading hours, and Friday–Saturday, which carried 35% of baseline demand. Added capacity was absorbed immediately by real, previously-unmet demand. The two-team sector-split model held simultaneous incidents without dropping coverage, and the only residual unattended demand sits on single-team night cover. ASG recommends the deployment pattern tested and evidenced in this pilot — two afternoon teams Sunday–Thursday and an enlarged combined Friday–Saturday team — as the baseline design for city-centre guardianship as CRL opening lifts footfall through late 2026, with night cover as the next capacity case.

## 13.  Data Sources
Figures are compiled from every daily patrol log for 1 June – 18 July 2026 (48 days), reviewed line by line, with duplicate and carried-over entries removed and cross-team double-counts consolidated; where a team’s written log was not returned for a shift that operated (nine of the 48 days, concentrated in the first three pilot weeks), its activity is included at the verified average level for equivalent fully-logged two-team days, rounded to whole events — the effect on headline totals is under one per cent. Totals reconcile exactly with the HOTC Weekly Data Reports for the period. Baseline and year-on-year figures are drawn from the weekly data reports for 9 March – 31 May 2026 and June–July 2025 respectively. The pre-pilot capacity figures (211 missed interventions, 29%) are from ASG’s January–April 2026 capacity analysis.
